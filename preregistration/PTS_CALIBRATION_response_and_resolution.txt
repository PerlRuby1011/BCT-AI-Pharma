3. THE ALERT-THRESHOLD SWEEP (Option 1) — DECISIVE RESULT
======================================================================

10 seeds x 230,000 transactions, Phase 6 data-quality settings.
Sensitivity analysis only; the operating point remains 0.75 and no
threshold was chosen on the basis of the value it produces.

  alert     BCT-AI      AI-Only      gap (pp)
  ------------------------------------------------
  0.60      50.91%       5.57%        +45.34
  0.65      57.85%      16.21%        +41.64
  0.70      64.49%      34.16%        +30.33
  0.75      71.40%      57.59%        +13.82   <- operating point
  0.80      78.24%      78.53%         -0.29   <- "target reached"
  0.85      85.31%      92.39%         -7.08
  0.90      92.47%      98.23%         -5.77

READ THE 0.80 ROW.

At alert=0.80, BCT-AI detection is 78.24% — it clears 78.2%. Any search
that used "exceeds 78.2%" as its stopping criterion terminates exactly
here and reports success. And at that same threshold AI-Only is 78.53%,
i.e. HIGHER. RQ2's entire comparative claim inverts at the moment the
target is met.

This is a stronger statement than "threshold tuning is dishonest". It
is: threshold tuning toward this target is self-defeating on its own
terms. The setting that buys the headline number destroys the result
the paper actually rests on.

Mechanism: AI-Only's score is tightly concentrated (S8 ~ 0.81 - 0.15s,
observed range [0.456, 0.973]), so it crosses a high threshold nearly
all at once; BCT-AI's provenance term spreads its distribution (S1
range [0.000, 1.000]). Above ~0.80 the entire AI-Only mass drops below
the line.

Substantive consequence for the paper: the 13.8 pp advantage is
specific to the 0.75 operating point and REVERSES above ~0.80. The
manuscript does not currently say this. It is a genuine qualification
of a live claim, and it is the one thing from this whole exercise that
may be worth adding.

4. CORRECTION TO THE OPTION 2 COST ESTIMATE
======================================================================

Your handoff estimates Option 2 at "~10x current compute budget", and
the manuscript's Future Work item 1 says "an estimated order-of-
magnitude increase in computational budget". I think that is too
pessimistic, and it matters because it may be deterring a decision.

The 28-minute figure for the 6-phase orchestrator run is dominated by
MODEL TRAINING (LSTM + CNN + Isolation Forest, six times over). The
detection metric itself is cheap: I re-ran all ten seeds at full
230,000-transaction scale in ~12 seconds.

Option 2 does not require retraining anything per seed. It requires:
  - the already-trained CNN and Isolation Forest (trained once), and
  - a forward pass per anomaly-labelled transaction:
    2,300 counterfeit rows/seed x 10 seeds = 23,000 CNN inferences,
    plus cold-chain sequences through the Isolation Forest.

23,000 forward passes through a 64x64 model is seconds of compute, not
hours. The real cost of Option 2 is DESIGN AND ENGINEERING time —
generating a packaging feature vector and a cold-chain reading sequence
per anomaly-labelled transaction that are distributionally consistent
with what the models were trained on — not GPU budget. My estimate is
hours of implementation, not an order of magnitude more compute.

That correction should probably propagate to the manuscript's Future
Work item 1 if Option 2 is not undertaken, since the paper currently
overstates the barrier to its own highest-priority follow-up.

I have NOT implemented Option 2. It needs the author's go-ahead,
because its result must be accepted in advance whatever it is —
including a value below 71.4%. Pre-registering the methodology before
seeing any output is the whole point; the moment it is run with a
target in mind it becomes the same operation as threshold tuning.
