======================================================================
1. WHY THIS EXPERIMENT EXISTS
======================================================================

The live-inference experiment established two things. Detection is now
causally coupled to classifier quality: degrading the CNN moves integrated
detection 92.29% -> 87.53% under clustered injection (4.76 pp, t(9)=19.7,
p=1.0e-08). And under UNIFORM injection the integrated channel cannot
respond at all: holding S8 across its entire physically reachable range
[0.38, 0.88] moves integrated detection by 0.11 pp in total.

The cause is a weight-transfer failure, not a model failure. Detection
requires (w1*S1 + w8*S8)/(w1+w8) < 0.75. With provenance derived from real
custody history S1 = 0.974 under uniform injection, so for Class A
(w1=0.25, w8=0.10) detection requires S8 < 0.185, which requires a negative
authenticity probability. The published weights were calibrated when
custody trust was itself a severity-linked proxy with slope 0.90 —
provenance was then the most discriminative signal and weighting it 2.5:1
was correct. It is no longer correct once provenance is real.

The question this experiment asks is whether ANY allocation of weight
between provenance and AI confidence restores responsiveness under uniform
injection without destroying the integrated framework's advantage over
AI-Only.

2. HYPOTHESES
======================================================================

H6 [RESTATED 2026-07-28 BEFORE APPROVAL, round-2 review. The original
    wording asked whether there exists a (w1, w8) allocation under which
    BOTH (a) responsiveness exceeds 1.0 pp under uniform injection AND
    (b) the integrated condition still beats AI-Only under clustering.

    Leg (a) IS NOW ANSWERED, and not by this sweep. The geometry diagnosis
    that withdrew R-C also settled it: responsiveness is monotone in
    r = w8/(w1+w8) and saturates at the maximum possible 100 pp for every
    r >= 0.4050, i.e. across ~60% of the grid. Leg (a) is satisfied
    trivially and abundantly. Continuing to pose it as a hypothesis would
    be pre-registering a question already on the record — which is its own
    small species of dishonesty, since a "confirmed" H6(a) would read as
    an experimental finding when it is a capacity calculation that
    preceded the run.]

H6 (RESTATED). The live question is entirely leg (b), now conditioned on
    (a): does ANY allocation with usable responsiveness under uniform
    injection keep the integrated condition ahead of AI-Only under
    clustered injection, at the published 0.75 threshold?
      Operationalized: among allocations with r >= 0.4050 (where
      responsiveness is known to be saturated), does at least one satisfy
      integrated detection > AI-Only detection under clustered injection?
    FALSIFIED IF: no such allocation exists — i.e. every allocation that
    can respond to classifier quality under uniform injection has already
    surrendered the integrated advantage under clustering.
    That outcome is the substantive architectural result: provenance and AI
    confidence could not then be combined by ANY single fixed linear
    weighting serving both regimes, and the framework would require either
    regime-dependent weights or a non-linear combination. It must be
    reported in those terms rather than as a null.

    NOTE ON WHERE THE ANSWER COMES FROM. Because (a) is settled by
    geometry and (b) is a detection comparison, this experiment's live
    question is now unavoidably a detection question. That does not make
    it illegitimate — it makes R-G and R-H load-bearing, since a detection
    comparison is exactly what must not leak into the abstract, the
    conclusion, or an RQ answer.

H7 (the responsive region trades against uniform-injection detection).
    Allocations that satisfy H6(a) will show LOWER integrated detection
    under clustered injection than the published weights do.
    Rationale: responsiveness requires the AI term to be able to move the
    composite across the threshold, which necessarily reduces the
    provenance term's ability to carry rows across it on its own.
    FALSIFIED IF: some allocation improves both simultaneously.

NOT A HYPOTHESIS: "some weighting produces detection above 90%", or above
any other number. Detection magnitude is an outcome to be reported, never
a criterion for selecting a weighting. See section 5.

======================================================================
3. PROTOCOL
======================================================================

3.1 SIGNAL CACHING. Weights enter only the PTS aggregation, never model
    inference. Therefore per-row S1 and S8 are computed ONCE per seed per
    injection mode using the frozen live-inference pipeline
    (evaluation/live_inference.py, unchanged), cached, and the weight grid
    is evaluated analytically over the cache. This makes the sweep cost
    minutes rather than hours and guarantees that every grid point sees
    numerically identical model outputs.

3.2 GRID. w1 and w8 are varied over the two-component simplex actually
    used by the detection path, in steps of 0.01, subject to
    w1 + w8 <= 1.0 and both > 0. Only their RATIO affects the outcome
    (compute_pts normalizes by the weight sum), so the reported surface is
    parameterized by r = w8 / (w1 + w8) over [0.01, 0.99] in steps of 0.01.
    The published Class A allocation is r = 0.286.

3.3 CONDITIONS. Every grid point is evaluated under BOTH uniform and
    clustered injection, on all ten seeds (42-51), 230,000 transactions
    per seed. No condition is dropped.

3.4 FROZEN. Alert threshold 0.75, quarantine threshold 0.50, anomaly
    injection, transaction generation, the provenance penalty (0.10), the
    burn-in split, and both trained models are held exactly as in the
    live-inference experiment. NO THRESHOLD IS TO BE ADJUSTED IN THIS
    EXPERIMENT FOR ANY REASON.

3.5 COUPLING MEASUREMENT. Reachable range at a grid point is computed by
    holding S8 constant across [0.38, 0.88] as in the verified sweep, not
    by re-running the degraded CNN. If a specific allocation is later
    reported as recommended, its coupling must be CONFIRMED by an actual
    degraded-CNN arm at that allocation before any coupling claim is made
    for it — a reachable-range calculation is a capacity measure, not an
    effect measure. This distinction is the one the review of the previous
    experiment turned on.

======================================================================
4. DECISION RULES
======================================================================

R-A The full surface is reported: detection under both injection modes and
    reachable range, across the entire grid. No region is omitted for
    being unflattering, and the surface is published as data alongside the
    manuscript.

R-B H6 is judged on whether ANY grid point satisfies both 2(a) and 2(b),
    stated plainly either way.

R-C [AMENDED 2026-07-28 BEFORE APPROVAL, following round-2 review. The
    original text read: "among allocations satisfying H6(a) and H6(b), the
    one MAXIMIZING REACHABLE RANGE under uniform injection ... If two
    allocations tie on reachable range, the one closer to the published
    r = 0.286 wins." That criterion is WITHDRAWN as structurally unsound.
    Reasons, both verified numerically before approval:

    (i) CORNER SOLUTION. Writing r = w8/(w1+w8), the composite is
        PTS = (1-r)*S1 + r*S8, and the S1-window within which a row can be
        swung by S8 across [0.38, 0.88] has width 0.5r/(1-r) — zero at
        r = 0, unbounded as r -> 1. Reachable range is therefore monotone
        non-decreasing in r, and maximizing it alone selects r = 1, which
        is PTS = S8: the AI-Only condition. An unconstrained
        responsiveness objective recommends deleting the blockchain term.

    (ii) WORSE THAN A CORNER: A PLATEAU. Measured against the real
        per-row S1 values over all ten seeds, reachable range under
        uniform injection is 0.25 pp at the published r = 0.286, 91.6 pp
        at r = 0.400, 95.3 pp at r = 0.4025, and exactly 100.00 pp for
        EVERY r from 0.4050 upward.
        [Onset corrected 2026-07-28 after round-2 re-verification on a
        finer grid. This paragraph first said the plateau ran "from
        r = 0.50", covering "half" the space; that was an artifact of a
        coarse sampling grid (0.40, 0.50, 0.60, ...). True onset is
        r = 0.4050, so the plateau covers 59.5% of the simplex, not 50%.
        The closed form agrees: saturation begins once even the
        highest-provenance row is swingable, at
        r = (S1_max - 0.75)/(S1_max - 0.38), which is 0.403 for
        S1_max ~ 1.0. The error made the finding sound milder than it is.]
        The objective is FLAT across roughly 60% of the parameter space,
        and informative over only about a tenth of it (0.38 to 0.405). It
        cannot discriminate among allocations on the plateau at all, so
        the tie-break would have performed the entire selection while the
        stated objective performed none of it. A tie-break is not a
        criterion.

    (iii) THE OBJECTIVE WAS NOT WELL DEFINED. "Reachable range" admits at
        least two reasonable readings that disagree materially: sweeping
        S8 at its observed per-row values gives 23.84 pp at the published
        allocation, while sweeping the interval endpoints gives 60.27 pp.
        Same allocation, same data, different number.

    (iv) H6(b) WAS LOAD-BEARING, NOT A FILTER. Because the objective
        climbs monotonically into the constraint, the selected allocation
        would have sat ON the H6(b) boundary — i.e. been fixed by a
        DETECTION comparison despite the stated objective being
        responsiveness. The reader check offered in section 5 of this
        document ("verify the recommendation is not the
        detection-maximizing one") would have passed while being
        substantially weaker than it appeared.]

R-C (REPLACEMENT) THE PRIMARY DELIVERABLE IS THE SURFACE, NOT A
    RECOMMENDED POINT. The experiment reports detection under both
    injection modes and responsiveness across the whole grid, and
    characterizes the trade-off between them. It does NOT nominate a
    single recommended allocation, because no criterion available to this
    experiment selects one honestly: responsiveness degenerates as shown
    above, and detection is disqualified by construction.

    If a later experiment wishes to recommend an operating point, it needs
    a criterion with an INTERIOR optimum — for example maximizing
    counterfeit/clean separation (AUC or Cohen's d) in the composite score
    under CLUSTERED injection, where both terms carry signal and the
    optimum is therefore genuinely interior rather than at a corner. That
    criterion must be pre-registered separately, with the corner-solution
    geometry above disclosed in advance so the choice cannot be presented
    as a discovery.

    Whatever is reported, the corner-solution property is stated in the
    write-up as geometry rather than as anyone's preference, so that a
    reader can see it was known before the sweep ran.

R-D The detection rate at the recommended allocation is reported as
    measured, including if it is lower than the 71.4% the paper reports
    under the published weights.

R-E The published weights REMAIN the paper's operating point. This
    experiment characterizes the weight surface; it does not retroactively
    re-specify the evaluation. Table VI, the ablation, the t-statistics
    and every headline number in the manuscript stay as measured under the
    published weights. Any recommended allocation is offered as guidance
    for future deployments and future work, in its own subsection, clearly
    marked as not the configuration under which this paper's results were
    obtained.

R-F Single execution. If an implementation defect is found after results
    are seen, the fix, the re-run and the discarded result are all recorded
    with the reason.

R-G [ADDED 2026-07-28 before approval, round-2 review.] NO DETECTION
    FIGURE FROM THIS SWEEP APPEARS IN THE ABSTRACT, THE CONCLUSION, OR ANY
    RESEARCH-QUESTION ANSWER. R-E protects Table VI and the reported
    results, but those are not the channel through which a flattering
    number would actually travel. This line of work began by being asked
    to reach 78.2%, then 90%; the abstract and the RQ answers are where
    such a number would be most useful and least scrutinized. Sweep
    figures are confined to the sweep's own subsection, always paired with
    their injection condition and their allocation.

R-H [ADDED 2026-07-28.] H6(b) IS LOAD-BEARING AND MUST BE REPORTED AS
    SUCH. It is not a sanity filter. Any allocation-dependent conclusion
    must state which of H6(a) and H6(b) is binding at the allocation
    discussed, because a conclusion resting on the constraint rather than
    the objective is a conclusion about detection, whatever the stated
    objective was.

======================================================================
5. THE CENTRAL RISK, STATED PLAINLY
======================================================================

This experiment sweeps a parameter that directly controls the headline
metric, immediately after a result that made the headline metric look bad
under one condition. That is the exact configuration in which outcome
selection happens, and it would be easy to dress up: the sweep will almost
certainly contain an allocation with detection above 71.4% under uniform
injection, and possibly one above 90% under clustered injection. Adopting
such a point because of its detection rate — however it is subsequently
justified — would invalidate this experiment and retroactively damage the
credibility of the two that preceded it.

The guards are now: R-C declines to nominate an allocation at all, because
the only non-detection criterion available degenerates (see R-C's amendment
notes); R-E keeps sweep results out of the paper's reported numbers; R-G
keeps them out of the abstract, conclusion and RQ answers, which is where
such a number would be most useful and least examined; R-H forces
disclosure of which condition is binding wherever an allocation-dependent
claim is made.

The reader check originally offered here — "verify the recommended
allocation is not the detection-maximizing one" — has been WITHDRAWN as
misleading. Round-2 review established that it would have passed while the
recommendation was in fact fixed by the H6(b) detection constraint. It is
replaced by: verify that no allocation is recommended, that no sweep
detection figure appears outside the sweep's own subsection, and that
Table VI is unchanged.

If a later experiment does nominate an allocation, and its
separation-maximizing choice coincides with the detection-maximizing one,
that coincidence must be stated explicitly rather than left for a reader to
notice.

A prior expectation, recorded now: the responsiveness-maximizing allocation
will place substantially MORE weight on AI confidence than the published
r = 0.286, and will show LOWER clustered-injection detection than 92.29%.
If the recommended allocation instead turns out to raise detection
everywhere, that is a surprising result and should be treated with more
suspicion, not less.

======================================================================
6. WHAT THIS EXPERIMENT CANNOT DO
======================================================================

It cannot revisit RQ1. RQ1's decision rule was fixed in the previous
pre-registration and concerns the primary live-inference arm under the
published weights. A weight sweep is a different experiment and does not
inherit that question.

It cannot establish coupling at any allocation on its own. Reachable range
is a capacity measure; a coupling claim requires a degraded-CNN arm at that
allocation (3.5).

It cannot resolve the synthetic-separability limitation, which bounds every
result in this line of work and is recorded in the manuscript's Limitations.

======================================================================
7. COST
======================================================================

Signal caching: one live-inference pass per injection mode over ten seeds,
approximately 30 minutes total at the measured 6.76 ms/row, reusing the
already-trained models. Grid evaluation over the cache: minutes. A
confirmatory degraded-CNN arm at the recommended allocation, if one is
reported, adds approximately 30 minutes.
