======================================================================
1. WHY THIS EXPERIMENT EXISTS
======================================================================

OPTION5 (executed 2026-08-24/25) tested empirical-Bayes shrinkage of
per-organization counterfeit-rate estimates. Result: zero effect, exactly,
on every one of 10 seeds for ARM4 (92.29%/12.34% TPR/FPR, identical to 4
decimal places before and after shrinkage) and every one of 10 seeds for
ARM1 (0.00%/0.00%, also identical).

The leading explanation: `compute_org_reputation()` clips the
excess-over-base-rate term to `PROVENANCE_EXCESS_CAP = 3.0` BEFORE the
penalty is applied (live_inference.py:108,135). Under clustered/
source-concentrated injection, the "hot" organizations' burn-in counterfeit
rates are far enough above the network base rate that both the raw
estimate and the shrunk estimate exceed this cap — so the clipped value,
and everything downstream of it, comes out identical either way. Shrinkage
was aimed at a stage of the pipeline (the rate estimate) that a later stage
(the cap) already overrides for the organizations that matter most.

This experiment tests that explanation directly by varying the cap itself,
instead of the estimate feeding it.

A genuine, stated-in-advance risk this experiment must respect: S1 is
computed as a PRODUCT of per-organization trust scores along a product's
custody chain (`compute_provenance_scores`, ~12 hops per chain). Loosening
or removing the cap allows any single low-trust hop to pull an entire
chain's S1 toward zero, REGARDLESS of how trustworthy the other ~11
organizations in that chain are. Under clustered injection, a meaningful
share of otherwise-clean products may pass through a "hot" organization at
some point in their chain. It is therefore NOT obvious that loosening the
cap reduces FPR — the multiplicative structure means it could just as
plausibly make more clean products collapse to a low S1, increasing FPR.
Both directions are stated as live possibilities below, not just the
favorable one.

======================================================================
2. HYPOTHESES, AND WHAT WOULD FALSIFY THEM
======================================================================

H8.1 (primary — cap sweep, directional but explicitly uncertain).
    As `PROVENANCE_EXCESS_CAP` increases from 3.0 (current) through 6.0,
    10.0, to uncapped (no clipping), ARM4's FPR and TPR will both move
    monotonically in ONE direction — but which direction is not confidently
    predictable in advance, for the reason stated in Section 1 (the
    multiplicative chain-collapse risk cuts against the naive "looser cap
    always means more discrimination" intuition).

    PREDICTION (stated with the uncertainty made explicit, not hedged after
    the fact): FPR decreases as the cap loosens. This is the "naive"
    direction — a fuller penalty on genuinely bad organizations should, on
    its face, sharpen the distinction between hot and cold organizations.
    We flag before running anything that the chain-multiplicative structure
    could reverse this, and if it does, THAT is the primary finding, not a
    failure of the experiment.

    FALSIFIED / REVERSED IF: FPR increases (rather than decreases) as the
    cap loosens. This would directly confirm the multiplicative-collapse
    mechanism as dominant, and would mean the excess cap is not merely
    insufficient but load-bearing — removing it makes specificity worse,
    and any future fix needs to address the multiplicative aggregation
    itself (e.g. a bounded/averaged combination instead of a raw product),
    not the per-organization scoring rule. This is written here, before the
    result is known, precisely so that a reversal cannot later be waved
    away as unexpected.

H8.2 (secondary — combining the loosened cap with OPTION5's shrinkage).
    At each loosened cap value, shrinkage (as defined in OPTION5) is
    applied on top. Because OPTION5 showed shrinkage is a no-op exactly
    when the cap dominates, shrinkage should start to matter once the cap
    stops saturating — i.e., the shrunk and unshrunk curves should diverge
    as the cap loosens, even though they were identical at cap=3.0.

    PREDICTION: at cap=10.0 and uncapped, shrunk FPR is lower than unshrunk
    FPR by a non-negligible margin (>=1.0 pp), unlike the exact-zero gap
    seen throughout OPTION5.

    FALSIFIED IF: shrunk and unshrunk remain identical (or nearly so) even
    at the loosest cap setting. That would mean the cap was never the sole
    explanation for OPTION5's null, and something else in the pipeline is
    also absorbing the correction — motivating a closer look at
    `compute_provenance_scores`'s log-sum aggregation itself, not just the
    two components tested so far.

H8.3 (positive control — ARM1 stays near-unchanged across the sweep).
    ARM1 (uniform injection) already measures 0.00%/0.00% at cap=3.0
    because no organization's excess is large to begin with under uniform
    injection. Loosening the cap should not manufacture a false-positive
    problem where OPTION5 already showed none exists.

    PREDICTION: ARM1's FPR and TPR stay within 1.0 pp of 0.00% at every
    cap value tested, shrunk or unshrunk.

    FALSIFIED IF: ARM1's FPR moves by more than 1.0 pp at any cap setting.
    That would indicate the cap parameter has unexpected effects even in
    the regime it was never meant to touch, and any cap change needs
    reworking before being trusted anywhere.

======================================================================
3. DESIGN — FROZEN ON APPROVAL
======================================================================

Single parameter swept, `PROVENANCE_EXCESS_CAP`, via the existing `cap`
argument to `compute_org_reputation()` (no new code path — the parameter
already exists and is already tested by OPTION2/OPTION4/OPTION5's default
of 3.0). Combined factorially with the existing `shrinkage` flag from
OPTION5. Everything else (CNN, Isolation Forest, transaction generation,
quarantine threshold, seeds) untouched.

  Cap values tested, chosen a priori (not tuned against any FPR outcome):
      3.0   — current/registered value, reference point, not re-run
              (already have ARM1/ARM4 baseline and shrunk from OPTION5)
      6.0   — 2x current
      10.0  — >3x current
      None  — uncapped (no clipping applied to excess at all)

  Shrinkage: OFF and ON at each of the three NEW cap values (6.0, 10.0,
  None) — 3 caps x 2 shrinkage settings x 2 conditions (ARM1, ARM4) = 12
  new arm/seed-set combinations, each run across the same 10 registered
  seeds (42-51) with the same trained CNN/Isolation Forest models (protocol
  section-4 reuse rule, unchanged).

  No-leakage rule, unchanged from OPTION5: shrinkage's internal parameter k
  is still derived automatically from each seed's own burn-in data. The cap
  values themselves are fixed by this document before any result is seen,
  not selected by scanning outcomes.

======================================================================
4. THE POWER TRAP — EXPLICIT GUARD
======================================================================

Same discipline as OPTION2/OPTION4/OPTION5. If the between-seed standard
deviation of a reported difference is large enough that its 95% CI includes
zero, that comparison is reported as inconclusive, not as a pass, regardless
of the point estimate. Given OPTION5's near-zero variance pattern held
throughout, near-zero variance here too should be reported as structural,
not as an inflated effect size.

======================================================================
7. WHAT THIS EXPERIMENT CANNOT SETTLE
======================================================================

It sweeps one scalar parameter (the cap) and re-tests one already-built
correction (shrinkage) against it. It does not test alternative chain
AGGREGATION rules (e.g., a bounded average instead of a product, or
per-hop confidence propagation) — if H8.1 reverses or H8.2 falsifies, that
is exactly the signal that aggregation-level redesign is the next
question, not something this experiment itself can answer. It also does
not revisit external validity; everything remains simulation-internal on
the same disclosed-synthetic transaction generator.
