9. WHY R-C'S OBJECTIVE IS PINNED BY A CONSTRAINT (detail for Q3 / A-1)
======================================================================

This is analytic, not empirical — no part of the weight sweep was run,
and nothing here anticipates its result.

Write r = w8 / (w1 + w8). Because compute_pts normalizes by the weight
sum, the detection rule is

    (1 - r) * S1  +  r * S8  <  0.75

Reachable range at a grid point is defined (protocol 3.5) as detection at
S8 = 0.38 minus detection at S8 = 0.88. Substituting, detection at fixed
S8 is P(S1 < (0.75 - r*S8) / (1 - r)), so the range is

    P( S1 < (0.75 - 0.38r)/(1-r) )  -  P( S1 < (0.75 - 0.88r)/(1-r) )

i.e. the probability mass of S1 inside a window whose width is

    (0.88 - 0.38) * r / (1 - r)  =  0.5r / (1 - r)

That width is 0 at r = 0 and increases without bound as r -> 1. At r = 0
the composite is S1 alone and cannot respond to the classifier at all
(range 0). At r = 1 it is S8 alone: detection is 100% at S8 = 0.38 and 0%
at S8 = 0.88, a range of 100 pp — the maximum attainable.

So the objective R-C maximizes is increasing in r across the whole grid,
and its unconstrained maximum is r = 1 — which is precisely the AI-Only
condition. An unconstrained R-C recommends deleting the blockchain.

H6(b) is therefore not a secondary sanity filter; it is the only thing
that makes R-C well-posed. And because the objective increases in r right
up to the constraint, the selected allocation will lie ON the H6(b)
boundary: the largest r at which the integrated condition still beats
AI-Only under clustered injection.

The consequence for the protocol's own credibility argument: section 5
offers the reader a check — "verify that the recommended allocation is not
the detection-maximizing one." That check will pass, but it is weaker than
it sounds, because the recommendation is fixed by the boundary of a
detection comparison even though the stated objective is responsiveness.
Better to say so in advance (amendment A-1) than to have a reviewer derive
